The problem Today

Satellite data is everywhere.
Understanding it isn’t.

Most remote-sensing AI arrives as isolated tools, each built for one predefined task. Getting an answer means knowing which sensor you are looking at, which model fits the question, how to prepare the imagery, and how to read what comes back.

And many operational questions cannot be answered from a single optical image at all. The information is spread across dates and across sensors.

Before a single question gets answered

  1. IMAGEGeoTIFF, 12 bands, unknown projection
  2. +SENSOROptical, multispectral or SAR
  3. +MODELOne network per task, per modality
  4. +WORKFLOWResample, co-register, speckle-filter
  5. +PARAMETERSThresholds, band maths, GSD

Multimodal Optical + SAR

Earth doesn’t speak
in one sensor.

Optical imagery carries spectrum and context. SAR carries structure, and acquires through cloud, day or night. Read together, over the same patch of ground, they answer questions neither can answer alone.

On its own

Blocked by cloud. Dark at night. Built-up and bare soil look alike.

Optical+SAR=Richer understanding

“Combining optical context with SAR structure improves interpretation of built-up and water-covered regions — high backscatter separates structures from the bare soil they resemble spectrally, and water’s near-zero return gives a boundary that haze cannot blur.”
